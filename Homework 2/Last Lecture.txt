Last Lecture

Randy Pausch’s The Last Lecture affected me because of the way he talked about life even though he knew that he did not have much time left. Instead of focusing only on his illness, he talked about his childhood dreams, the people who influenced him, and the lessons he learned throughout his life. I think that made his message more meaningful because he was not just telling people to “live life to the fullest.” He was explaining what that actually looked like in his own life.

One message that affected me the most was his idea about “brick walls.” He explained that obstacles are not always there to stop us, but sometimes they show us how badly we want something. I identified with this because there have been times when things did not happen easily for me, and I had to keep pushing myself instead of waiting for things to work out on their own. I think it is easy to become discouraged when something feels unfair or difficult, but his message reminded me that obstacles do not always mean that something is impossible.

I also connected with his message about appreciating the people around you and not wasting the time that you have. Knowing that he was giving this lecture while facing a terminal illness made that lesson hit harder. We usually live as if we have unlimited time to accomplish things, fix relationships, or do what makes us happy, but that is not guaranteed.

Overall, *The Last Lecture* made me think about how I handle challenges and how I want to use my time. I liked that Randy did not present life as something that is always easy or positive. Instead, he showed that we can still choose how we respond to difficult circumstances and continue working toward the things that matter to us.
